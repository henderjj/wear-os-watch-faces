# Earth Day & Night: project guide

An analogue Wear OS watch face showing the Earth from space, lit as it is right now. Daylight, twilight and the night side with city lights follow the real position of the Sun through the day and the seasons. Built for a Galaxy Watch 6 with **Watch Face Format v1**, so it runs on Wear OS 4 and later.

![The face through the day](images/day-cycle.png)

*08:00, 13:00, 18:30 and 23:02 BST on 8 October 2026, rendered from the shipped XML.*

**Current version:** 1.2.1 (versionCode 4)

For the general approach (working with an AI agent, the format's pitfalls, the check-and-test loop), see [docs/agentic-wear-os-watch-faces.md](../../docs/agentic-wear-os-watch-faces.md).

---

## 1. Features

- **Live Earth.** The view is centred on 0°N 0°E (the equator at Greenwich), with Europe and the UK towards 12 o'clock.
  - The sunlit half shows NASA Blue Marble imagery.
  - The night half shows NASA city lights, with the continents faintly visible.
  - A soft twilight band separates them.
- **The day/night line is correct for the date.** It uses solar declination for the seasons and the equation of time.
- **Day and date windows** with steel frames and condensed capitals: **THU** at 9 o'clock and **08 OCT** at 3 o'clock, or **THU 08 OCT** in one window at 6 o'clock.
- **Four hand styles in four metals**, plus a sweeping seconds hand in five colours.
- **Always-on display:** a dimmed night globe, dial, date, and hour and minute hands. The seconds hand is hidden.

![Option combinations](images/options.png)

### Settings (long-press the face → Customise)

| Setting | Type | Options (default first) |
|---|---|---|
| Hand style | List `handStyle` | Dauphine, Sword (with lume insert), Baton, Skeleton (open frame) |
| Hand colour | Colour `handTint` | White, Steel, Gold, Rose gold |
| Seconds hand colour | Colour `accentTint` | Orange, Red, Yellow, Blue, White |
| Seconds hand | Boolean `seconds` | On, Off |
| Date | List `dateStyle` | Day and date apart, One window at 6, Off |

## 2. Project layout

This face lives in `faces/earth-day-night/` of the repository. It's a self-contained Android Studio project, and it uses the shared scripts in the repository's top-level `tools/` folder.

```
faces/earth-day-night/
├── README.md                     overview, settings, quick rebuild
├── GUIDE.md                      this guide
├── images/                       pictures used by the README and this guide
├── inputs/                       NASA textures, the Barlow Condensed font and its licence
├── tools/                        scripts that generate the watch face (section 5)
├── watchface/
│   ├── build.gradle.kts          applicationId uk.earthface.daynight, minSdk/targetSdk 33
│   └── src/main/
│       ├── AndroidManifest.xml   hasCode="false", WFF version property = 1
│       └── res/
│           ├── raw/watchface.xml        the watch face (generated, about 73 KB)
│           ├── values/strings.xml       names shown in Customise (generated)
│           ├── xml/watch_face_info.xml  preview image + Editable=true
│           └── drawable-nodpi/          113 PNGs (generated, about 1.2 MB)

```

**Don't hand-edit `watchface.xml`, `strings.xml` or the PNGs.** Change the scripts in `tools/` and rebuild (section 5).

## 3. How it works

### 3.1 Scene layers (bottom to top)

| # | Layer | Images | Always-on |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Starfield | `space` | hidden |
| 2 | Atmosphere glow at the limb | `atmosphere` | 25% |
| 3 | **Night Earth**: city lights, dimmed continents | `earth_night` (392×392) | 55% |
| 4 | **Day Earth**, clipped by the terminator mask (3.3) | `earth_day` + `mask_00`…`mask_36` | hidden |
| 5 | Minute track: 60 ticks, heavier at the hours | `dial` | 59% |
| 6 | Date windows, by `dateStyle` | `frame_*`, `day_*`, `dayr_*`, `dig_*`, `mon_*` | 75% |
| 7 | Hour and minute hands, by `handStyle` (3 layers each, 3.5) | `halo_*`, `body_*`, `detail_*` | shown |
| 8 | Centre cap in the hand colour | `cap_base`, `cap_top`, `cap_ring` | shown |
| 9 | Seconds hand and pin in the accent colour, if `seconds` is on | `halo_second`, `body_second`, `pin_ring`, `pin_dot` | hidden |

The Earth images are orthographic views rendered from equirectangular NASA textures. They include a slight blue haze towards the edge on the day side and a faint blue rim on the night side.

### 3.2 Where the Sun is

These expressions are generated by `tools/wff_expr.py` and evaluated by the watch as it draws each frame.

| Quantity | Expression (degrees unless noted) |
|---|---|
| UTC hours | `T = (UTC_TIMESTAMP mod 86 400 000) / 3 600 000` |
| Year angle from the March equinox | `B = 0.9863 × (DAY_OF_YEAR − 81)` |
| Equation of time (minutes) | `EoT = 9.87 sin 2B − 7.53 cos B − 1.5 sin B` |
| Hour angle at the view centre | `H = 15 (T − 12) + EoT/4 + LON0` |
| Declination | `δ = 23.44 sin B` |

The Sun's direction is then given in screen terms. `x` points east, `y` points north, and `z` points out of the screen towards you:

```
Sx = −cos δ · sin H
Sy =  sin δ · cos LAT0 − cos δ · sin LAT0 · cos H
Sz =  sin δ · sin LAT0 + cos δ · cos LAT0 · cos H
```

The integer-millisecond modulo keeps precision. Using `UTC_TIMESTAMP` means the result doesn't depend on the watch's time zone or daylight saving.

### 3.3 The day/night mask

Every point on the visible disk is lit by `illum = u·Sx + v·Sy + z·Sz`, where `(u, v, z)` is the point on the sphere. Rotate the screen so the Sun lies to the right, and the shape of the lit area depends only on **one angle: how far the Sun is from the viewing direction**, `acos(Sz)`.

- **37 masks** (`mask_00` to `mask_36`) are pre-rendered for that angle at 0°, 5°, … 180°. They're stored at half resolution and scaled up, which is fine because their edges are soft.
- Each mask's opacity uses a smooth step from `illum = −0.10` to `+0.26`. That runs from the Sun about 6° below the horizon (civil twilight) to about 15° above it.
- At any moment, the **two nearest masks** are drawn with blended opacity, using `255 · clamp(1 − (idx − j), 0, 1) · clamp((idx − j + 1)·1000, 0, 1)`. So the shape changes smoothly instead of jumping between steps.
- The `terminator` group is **rotated** so its lit side faces the Sun: `angle = acos(Sx / √(Sx²+Sy²)) · sign(−Sy)`, measured clockwise from 3 o'clock.
- That group has `renderMode="MASK"` inside the `daylight` group, so it clips `earth_day`. Where the mask is clear, `earth_night` shows through from underneath.

**Accuracy.** Compared with an independent precise solar calculation, the mask is off by 0.2–1.1% on average across nine test times through the year. At the 99th percentile the difference is under 15%, and it only occurs in the soft twilight band.

### 3.4 Date windows

Day and month names from the watch (`DAY_OF_WEEK_S`, `MONTH_S`) depend on its language and can't be styled. Instead, every word and digit is a **pre-rendered image** in Barlow Condensed SemiBold, with letter spacing, picked by number through `Condition`s:

| Part | Selected by | Images |
|---|---|---|
| Weekday | `[DAY_OF_WEEK] == 1…7` (1 = Sunday) | `day_1…7` (centred) or `dayr_1…7` (right-aligned for the single window) |
| Tens digit | `([DAY] - ([DAY] % 10)) == 0 / 10 / 20 / 30` | `dig_0…3` |
| Units digit | `([DAY] % 10) == 0…9` | `dig_0…9` |
| Month | `[MONTH] == 1…12` | `mon_1…12` |

Every value has its own exact-match rule, so **exactly one rule in each Condition is true** at any time. There's no `Default`, and nothing depends on the order of the rules. This matters because WFF shows every `Compare` whose expression is true, not just the first.

Version 1.2 used overlapping ranges for the tens digit (`[DAY] < 10`, `< 20`, `< 30`, otherwise 3). On the watch, that showed **31 OCT on 21 October**. All the expressions use integer arithmetic only, which avoids WFF's quirks with comparing floats. Frames (`frame_day`, `frame_date`, `frame_full`) are dark glass with a bevelled steel edge. Positions come from `tools/generated/date_layout.json`. The split windows' outer edges sit 184 px from the centre, and the single window is centred 147 px below it.

### 3.5 Hands and colours

**Version 5 is the first WFF version that allows one setting inside another.** In v1, the hand colour therefore can't be a list nested inside each hand style. Instead:

- `handStyle` is a top-level `ListConfiguration`. Each option holds **six `AnalogClock`s**: for both the hour and minute hand, a **halo** (soft black shadow), a **body** and a **detail** layer, in that order.
- The **body** is pure white and tinted with `tintColor="[CONFIGURATION.handTint.0]"`.
- The **detail** layer holds the darker right-hand facet (black at 26% opacity) and, on the Sword, the lume insert with its dark outline.
- The detail layer is never tinted, so the hands look the same whatever blend mode the watch uses for tinting.
- The cap and the seconds hand use the same layering, with `handTint` and `accentTint` respectively.

| Hand | Length from pivot | Tail |
|---|---|---|
| Hour | 112 px | 20 px |
| Minute | 172 px | 22 px |
| Seconds | 188 px | 36 px, with a counterweight; sweeps at 15 Hz |

## 4. Install or update on the watch

The full steps are in [docs/install-on-galaxy-watch.md](../../docs/install-on-galaxy-watch.md). In short:
1. Open the `faces/earth-day-night` folder in Android Studio and let Gradle sync.
2. On the watch, turn on Developer options, ADB debugging and Wireless debugging.
3. Run `adb pair` (first time only), then `adb connect` with the IP address and port shown on the watch.
4. In the Run configuration, set **Launch** to **Nothing**, then press **Run ▶**. The new version replaces the old one in place.

Errors from the watch face runtime can be read with:

```powershell
$adb = "$env:LOCALAPPDATA\Android\Sdk\platform-tools\adb.exe"
& $adb logcat --pid=$(& $adb shell pidof -s com.google.wear.watchface.runtime)
```

## 5. Rebuilding from source

### 5.1 Inputs
`tools/build.py` uses three files from `inputs/` by default. You only need to pass `--day`, `--night` or `--font` to try different ones.

| Input | Where to get it | Licence |
|---|---|---|
| Day texture, 4096×2048 equirectangular | `example/img/earth-blue-marble.jpg` in [vasturiano/three-globe](https://github.com/vasturiano/three-globe) (NASA Blue Marble) | NASA imagery, public domain |
| Night texture, 4096×2048 | `example/img/earth-night.jpg` in the same repo (NASA Earth at Night) | NASA imagery, public domain |
| Date font | `ofl/barlowcondensed/BarlowCondensed-SemiBold.ttf` in [google/fonts](https://github.com/google/fonts) | SIL OFL 1.1 |

### 5.2 Build
You need Python 3.9 or later with `numpy` and `pillow`. Run these from `faces/earth-day-night`:

```bash
pip install numpy pillow
python tools/build.py
python ../../tools/validate_wff.py watchface/src/main/res/raw/watchface.xml --version 1
```

The validator needs git and Java. On Windows it uses the Java bundled with Android Studio. Its first run downloads and compiles Google's validator into the repository's `external/` folder, which git ignores.

This takes about 30 seconds, then:
1. renders the Earth, masks, starfield, glow and dial (`render_assets.py`)
2. renders the hands, cap and seconds artwork (`hands.py`)
3. renders the date frames, words and digits, and writes the layout (`date_assets.py`)
4. replaces everything in `drawable-nodpi/`
5. generates `watchface.xml` and `strings.xml` (`make_xml.py`)
6. renders `preview.png` with the default settings at 10:08:32
7. runs the checks (5.3)

Rebuilding from unchanged inputs reproduces the shipped v1.2 files exactly: identical XML and pixel-identical images.

### 5.3 Checks

| Script | What it checks |
|---|---|
| `../../tools/wff_lint.py` (shared) | No setting is nested inside another; each `CONFIGURATION` reference points to a colour setting; no missing or unused drawables or strings |
| `check_dates.py` | For every day from 2026 to 2028, in both layouts: exactly one rule is true in each date Condition, and the images drawn spell the right date |
| `check_faces.py` | Renders nine times of year and compares the day/night mask with `solar.py`. Previews go to `tools/generated/previews/` |
| `../../tools/wff_sim.py` (shared) | The renderer the checks use. It reads `watchface.xml` and draws any time, setting combination or always-on view: `python ../../tools/wff_sim.py watchface/src/main/res/raw/watchface.xml watchface/src/main/res/drawable-nodpi out.png 2026-10-08T10:08:32 1 handStyle=sword handTint=2 dateStyle=single` |
| `../../tools/validate_wff.py` (shared) | The XML against Google's official schema for format version 1 |

## 6. Common changes

All of these mean editing a file in `tools/` and then running `build.py`. Increase `versionCode` and `versionName` in `watchface/build.gradle.kts` before installing.

| Change | Where |
|---|---|
| **Centre the view somewhere else** (e.g. over London) | `params.py`: `LAT0`, `LON0`. The masks don't need to change; the expressions and Earth images follow automatically. |
| Wider or narrower twilight band | `params.py`: `LO`, `HI` (cosine of the solar zenith angle at the night and day ends) |
| Size of the globe | `params.py`: `EARTH_R` (196 px on a 450 px canvas) |
| Night side brighter or darker; city light colour | `render_assets.py` → `render_earth()` |
| Add a hand colour | `hands.py` → `METALS`: add `"4": ("Blue steel", "#FF7FA6D8")` |
| Add a seconds colour | `hands.py` → `ACCENTS` |
| Add a hand style | `hands.py`: write a drawing function that takes `(c, L, T, w, P)` and uses `P["light"]`, `P["dark"]`, `P["lume"]` and `P["ink"]`. Register it in `DRAW` and `STYLES`. |
| Hand lengths and widths | `hands.py` → `GEOM`; seconds hand in `render_seconds()` |
| Date font, size or letter spacing | `date_assets.py` → `SIZE`, `TRACK`, or pass a different `--font` (or replace the file in `inputs/`) |
| Date window positions | `date_assets.py` → `edge` (split windows) and `yc` (single window) |
| Date in another language | `date_assets.py` → `DAYS`, `MONTHS`. Keep the order: `DAYS` starts with Sunday. |
| Default settings | `make_xml.py` → `DEFAULTS` |
| Setting names shown on the watch | `make_xml.py` → `strings`, `DATE_STYLES`; `hands.py` → `STYLES`, `METALS`, `ACCENTS` |

**Don't nest one setting inside another setting's option** in WFF v1. For example, don't put a `ListConfiguration` inside a `ListOption`. The customise screen closes when the user changes that setting, and `wff_lint.py` reports it.

## 7. Known limitations

- **Testing so far:** a Galaxy Watch 6 only. Other round Wear OS 4+ watches should work but haven't been tried. The memory footprint hasn't been measured with Google's tool, because that needs a built APK.
- **Accuracy:** the mask moves in 5° steps, blended between steps. Close to the twilight band it can be a few pixels off a true calculation.
- **Clouds:** the imagery is a cloud-free composite, so there's no live weather.
- **Date language:** always English capitals by design. Change `DAYS` and `MONTHS` for another language.
- **Midnight:** for the minutes between local midnight and UTC midnight, the seasonal calculation uses the local day of the year. The resulting error is far below what you could see.
